# Design notes toward a CAD model and simulator

Running log. Append, do not rewrite: each note has an ID, a date and a status
(`adopted` / `proposed` / `open` / `retired`). Numbers live in
[`design/nf-cell-v0.yaml`](../design/nf-cell-v0.yaml); if a number here disagrees with the YAML, the
YAML wins.

## 1. Conventions

- **Axes:** right-handed. `z` is the stack normal (growth direction, heat and fuel flow), `x`,`y`
  in-plane. Origin at the centre of the reaction site of pixel (0,0), on the mid-plane of the
  reaction layer.
- **Units by level:** ångström at site level, nanometre at pixel level, micrometre at tile level and
  above. SI inside solvers. Energies in eV/keV/MeV, never joules, in design files.
- **Isotopes are explicit everywhere.** Specs and prose use `D`, `11B`, `7Li`, `12C`. Atomistic files
  keep the chemical symbol (so standard tools read them) and carry a per-atom `mass_number` column
  (2 = deuterium, 0 = natural mix). A model that cannot distinguish H from D is not a model of this
  device.
- **Every parameter carries provenance:** `measured`, `computed` (with script), `literature` (with
  citation) or `assumed`. Assumed values are the to-do list.

## 2. Level-of-detail hierarchy

One model, six nested levels. Each level is a parametric object that instantiates the level below and
exports to the solver that works at its scale.

| Level | Object | Size | Contents | Representation | Solver |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 | **Site** | 0.5–5 nm | fuel pair or cluster in its host cage; 10²–10⁴ atoms, atom-exact | extended XYZ / CIF, with isotope and charge tags | DFT, path-integral MD, TDDFT |
| L1 | **Antenna / cavity** | 5–50 nm | plasmonic gap, phononic cavity around one site | atomistic near the gap, continuum outside | FDTD/BEM electromagnetics, MD |
| L2 | **Pixel** | 100–500 nm | site + antenna + photon feed + local collector + fuel channel | parametric solids (CSG), layer stack | EM, drift-diffusion, heat |
| L3 | **Tile** | 10–100 µm | 10⁴–10⁶ pixels, power bus, fuel manifold, helium vent, detector channel | array + routing; GDSII/OASIS layers | circuit, transport Monte Carlo, thermal FEM |
| L4 | **Die** | 1–10 mm | tiles, drivers, readout | layout | circuit, thermal |
| L5 | **Cell / stack** | mm–cm | dies, heat spreaders, shielding, package | STEP | thermal, radiation transport, safety |

The CAD kernel question (DN-009) is which tool holds L2–L5. L0–L1 are atomistic files generated by
code, not drawn.

## 3. NF-cell v0: baseline geometry

A deliberately simple first cell, chosen so that Phase 1 experiment E1 and the first CAD render are
the same object. D-D fuel, because v0 is a laboratory instrument and D-D events are easy to count.
A drawing generated from the spec is in [`design/nf-cell-v0.svg`](../design/nf-cell-v0.svg)
(`python3 cad/render_cell.py`), and the atom-exact site S1 is in
[`design/sites/S1-PdD-vacancy.xyz`](../design/sites/S1-PdD-vacancy.xyz) (`python3 cad/gen_site.py`).

```
  z ↑        D⁺ beam (E1)  ↓ ↓ ↓          vacuum
    │      ┌──────┐ ┌──────┐
    │      │ PdDx │·│ PdDx │         L1: bowtie dimer, 30 nm prisms, 10 nm thick,
    │      └──────┘ └──────┘             gap 1.0 nm  ← reaction site (hot spot ~1–40 nm³)
    │   ───────────────────────────  2 nm hBN spacer (deuterium diffusion barrier)
    │   ═══════════════════════════  SiN waveguide, 200 nm (runs along x, into the page; TE, field along y)
    │   ···························  SiO₂ lower cladding, 1 µm
    │   ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░  intrinsic ¹²C diamond, 15 µm  ← collector / detector
    │   ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓  p⁺ diamond contact, heat spreader
    └──────────────────────────────→ y      pixel pitch 200 nm → 2.5 × 10⁹ sites/cm²
```

Why these choices:

- **PdDₓ as both fuel host and antenna.** Pd nanoparticles have a plasmon resonance that shifts with
  hydrogen loading (the basis of optical hydrogen sensors), so the same structure hosts the fuel,
  concentrates the light, and reports its own loading state optically. It is also the host with the
  most screening data. Pd is a lossy plasmonic metal; an Al or Ag antenna with a PdD insert is variant
  v0-b.
- **Diamond for everything else.** Carbon is where mechanosynthesis is most advanced; diamond is a
  radiation detector, has colour-centre single-photon emitters, conducts heat better than any other
  solid, and can be made isotopically pure. An all-carbon platform plus one metal is the smallest
  chemistry set that covers site, optics, capture and heat.
- **Sites on top, optics underneath.** The antennas are the top surface, so a keV beam (E1) and gas or
  plasma loading can reach them. Light arrives from below through the waveguide, and MeV products pass
  down through ~1 µm of optics into the diamond detector with little loss. Fuel arrives by beam
  implantation in E1, and by lateral Pd bus lines from an edge reservoir (which also allow
  electrochemical loading) in beamless operation. The hBN spacer keeps deuterium out of the optics.
- **Precedent for the geometry.** A single Pd nanoparticle placed in the nanofocus of a gold antenna,
  read out optically as it takes up hydrogen, was built in 2011 (Liu et al., *Nat. Mater.* 10, 631).
  v0 is that structure with deuterium, a detector underneath and a beam on top.

## 4. Catalogues

### 4.1 Site candidates (L0)

| ID | Site | Fuel | Why | Main doubt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | D pair at a Pd monovacancy / vacancy cluster in PdD | D-D | Most data; vacancies are where theory and the 2026 "defects" hint point | Equilibrium D-D spacing is worse than in D₂ |
| S2 | Interstitial H in ⁷LiH (rock-salt, Li-H 2.04 Å) | p-⁷Li | Stoichiometric crystal with fuel pairs as nearest neighbours; aneutronic; B_G = 88 | Insulator, so low static screening |
| S3 | Borophane / hydrogenated h-¹¹BN sheets | p-¹¹B | Atomically thin p-B crystal exists (borophane, 2021); product fuel | B_G = 150, the hardest barrier |
| S4 | D₂ in a carbon-vacancy cage in ¹²C diamond | D-D | Stiff lattice, high phonon frequencies, monolithic with the platform | Insulator; D is hard to load |
| S5 | Endohedral cage, D₂@C₆₀, in a metallic fulleride crystal | D-D | Atom-exact site made by chemistry in bulk; cage modes at 8–47 THz are optically addressable | No reason from known physics to expect enhancement; it is the simulator's calibration system |
| S6 | ErD₃ / TiD₂ nano-inclusions | D-D | NASA LCF hosts, 10²³ D/cm³ | Contested replication |
| S7 | Hydrogen-loaded PdLiₓ, or H at a vacancy next to Li in Pd | p-⁶Li, p-⁷Li | The only product-fuel pair already measured inside palladium, with sample-dependent enhancement | Needs 7–12 keV of screening at contact; ~1 keV reported |
| S8 | D₂ (or dideuterium) in a deuterium-packed Pd vacancy, ordered as a vacancy superlattice (Pd₃VacD₄) | D-D | The configuration the Hagelstein and Metzler papers require; a crystal in which every cell is a site | Whether the ordered phase exists and holds molecular D₂ is itself conjectural |

### 4.2 Photon delivery (L1–L2)

| ID | Scheme | Acts through | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Waveguide-fed plasmonic gap, CW or ps pulses | electrons (transient screening) | v0 baseline; tests the Google/UMD patent mechanism |
| P2 | Two-colour difference-frequency drive at 8–20 THz | lattice (optical phonons) | E3; needs two diode lines per tile, p-polarised |
| P3 | Per-site single-photon emitter in a picocavity (4 V/nm per photon) | electrons, quantum regime | Phase 2; for photon-number-resolved experiments |
| P4 | Low-β dielectric laser accelerator, period βλ = 6–70 nm | ion kinetic energy | Track A/D ion driver; fs pulses, not diodes |
| P5 | Acoustic drive: on-chip SAW/BAW transducer, or laser pulses at MHz–GHz repetition launching coherent acoustic phonons | lattice (uniform acoustic mode) | What the 2025 Hagelstein model actually calls for; their numbers imply ~4 mW out per watt in |
| P6 | Write-once: light switches a site into a long-lived active state | electrons or lattice, then nothing | The only optical scheme the photon budget allows at battery rates (DN-027) |
| P7 | Mid-infrared drive of the fuel pair's own stretch (LiH-type sites) | the pair's dipole, q_eff ≈ 0.5 e | Available only for unlike pairs (DN-028) |

### 4.3 Capture (L2–L5)

| ID | Scheme | Efficiency expectation | Lifetime issue |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Diamond p-i-n / Schottky junction | single-digit to ~20 % | Displacement damage; fine as a *detector* in Phases 1–2 |
| C2 | Direct charge collection of alphas against a retarding field | high in principle | Needs MV-scale potentials; 1 V/nm × 10 µm is only 10 kV |
| C3 | Secondary-electron cascade in nano-layered metal/insulator stacks | unknown | Speculative; model in Phase 2 |
| C4 | Thermal: thermoelectric at small ΔT | 5–10 % | Robust; the default if the reaction makes only heat |

### 4.4 Fuel and ash

- Feed by permeation through Pd or through 2D proton-conducting membranes from a gas or hydride
  reservoir. Fuel mass is negligible; design the feed for control, not capacity.
- Helium: 2.5 cm³ STP per watt-year for p-¹¹B. Every tile needs a vent path to the edge. Helium
  bubbles are what destroys hydrides and metals under alpha load.
- Tritium and neutrons from D-D make v0 a licensed laboratory device.

## 5. Scaling relations worth remembering

- Site density at 200 nm pitch: 2.5 × 10⁹ cm⁻². For 1 mW/cm² per layer from D-D (3.65 MeV average):
  0.7 events per site per second.
- A 40 nm³ hot spot in PdD holds ~2400 deuterons. At 200 nm pixel pitch and 20 µm layer pitch a
  40 nm³ hot spot fills 5 × 10⁻⁸ of the volume (a 1 nm³ picocavity, 10⁻⁹), so a hot-spot design needs
  a per-pair rate 10⁷–10⁹ times higher than a design that activates the whole crystal.
  **Localised drive only wins if the enhancement is extremely non-linear in field.**
  Otherwise volumetric phonon pumping (P2) is the better bet.
- Layer pitch in z is set by alpha range (6–12 µm), so power per volume ≈ power per layer / 20 µm.
- Break-even photon budget: N_γ ≈ η_emitter · E_fus / E_photon ≈ 10⁶, or ~10⁵ after capture losses.
- Space charge allows ~5 × 10¹⁹ bare ions/cm³ in a 10 nm trap for only ~4 V of self-potential (about
  26 ions). Confining 100 keV ions needs 100 kV across the structure: 10 nm at 10 V/nm (the atomic
  limit) or 100 nm at 1 V/nm (dielectric breakdown of hBN/diamond). Track D optics are 0.1–1 µm
  objects with atom-exact surfaces.

## 6. CAD data model

```
Project
 └─ Cell            (L5)  package, stack order, thermal/safety envelope
     └─ Die         (L4)  tile array, drivers
         └─ Tile    (L3)  pixel lattice vectors, buses, manifolds, vents
             └─ Pixel (L2)  layer stack + placed components
                 ├─ Antenna (L1)  geometry params, material, gap
                 │   └─ Site (L0)  host lattice, defect, fuel atoms, isotopes
                 ├─ PhotonFeed     waveguide / emitter, wavelength(s), polarisation
                 ├─ Collector      junction or thermal element
                 └─ FuelChannel    membrane, reservoir link
```

- **Source of truth:** a parametric text spec (YAML now) under version control. Geometry is always
  *generated* from it, never hand-edited, so every simulation is reproducible from a commit hash.
- **Exports:** L0–L1 → extended XYZ / CIF / LAMMPS data; L2–L5 → STEP through CadQuery
  (`cad/build_step.py`), each file at a declared scale (DN-030); GDSII/OASIS for lithography later.
- **Variants:** a site library with stable IDs (S1…), so a tile can be an array of *different* sites.
  Combinatorial chips are the experimental strategy from Phase 2, and the CAD model must express them
  natively.
- **Round trip:** measured per-site event rates are stored against the same IDs.

## 7. Simulator architecture

Multi-scale, with explicit hand-offs. Each arrow is a file format and a unit test.

| Stage | Question | Method (candidate tools) | Hands off |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Structure | Where do the atoms sit? | DFT (Quantum ESPRESSO, GPAW), ML potentials | relaxed L0 geometry, phonons |
| 2 Nuclear quantum motion | D-D pair distribution at short range | path-integral MD (i-PI) | g(r) down to ~10 pm, tunnelling prefactor |
| 3 Electron response | Screening energy; electronic stopping; driven plasmon density | real-time TDDFT | Uₑ(site, t), Sₑ(channel) |
| 4 Nuclear rate | Events per pair per second | S-factor × penetrability with stage 2–3 inputs; `sim/baseline.py` is its seed | Λ, branching |
| 5 Photonics | Field at the site per input photon | FDTD/BEM (Meep) | E(r, ω), absorbed power |
| 6 Transport | Where do products and heat go? | Monte Carlo (Geant4 / OpenMC), SRIM-class stopping | deposited energy, damage, dose |
| 7 Device | Electricity out | drift-diffusion, circuit | Q_site → Q_cell |
| 8 Process | Yield versus placement error | statistical sampling over stage 1–7 | tolerance budget for fabrication |

Implemented so far: stages 2 and 4 on model potentials (`sim/pair_fusion.py`), the beam-side baseline
(`sim/baseline.py`), and stage 7 run backwards as requirements (`sim/cell_model.py`). Stage 1 and 3
(first-principles structure and electron response at a real site) are the next physics step.

Rules:

- Stage 4 is the only place a speculative mechanism may enter, as an explicit, named multiplier with a
  citation. The baseline is never edited to accommodate a hypothesis.
- Validation benchmarks B1–B6 (roadmap Phase 0) are regression tests.
- First deliverable is not a fusion prediction. It is B2: screening energy by host metal, because it
  is measured, disputed and computable.

## 9. Decisions and open questions

Decided by the owner, 2026-11-20:

1. Product fuels: p-¹¹B or p-⁷Li (DN-024). D-D remains the laboratory fuel.
2. Light sources are shared; mass fabrication should plan on separate many-to-one laser units (DN-025).
3. Physics first, with a basic device-level cell model kept going alongside (DN-029).
4. Code-CAD with STEP export to Bldrs; missing viewer capabilities become feature requests (DN-030).

Open, in the order they block work:

1. Given DN-024, should the product architecture be re-centred on Track D (p-¹¹B in a recirculating
   beam) with Track B kept as the deuterium science programme? For p-¹¹B the lattice is 125 orders
   short on reported screening while the beam route sits near break-even on known physics, so the
   comparison favours the beam. Neither is shown to work (DN-036). Proposed: decide after a trap
   simulation study, which is cheap and can be run now.
2. Is natural lithium (which is effectively p-⁶Li fuel) acceptable alongside p-⁷Li?
3. First atomistic target: D₂ in a palladium vacancy (S1/S8), or hydrogen beside lithium in palladium
   (S7)? S1 has the data; S7 is the product fuel.
4. Is the 100 µW/cm³ sensor tier an acceptable first product target? It is the only tier where capture,
   burn-up, helium and heat are all benign (DN-032).
