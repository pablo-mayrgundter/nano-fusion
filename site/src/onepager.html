NanoFusion: a fusion battery, one reaction site at a time

A crystal of atom-exact reaction sites, each holding one fuel pair, switched by light from a shared laser. The study at a glance: what it would take, what is ruled out, and how to find out. Modelling only, no hardware.

The idea

Hydrogen and boron-11 fuse into three helium nuclei and almost nothing else, releasing 19 kWh per milligram. Burn that in a chip instead of a plasma: fuel pairs held one to a cage in a designed crystal, 2.5 × 10¹⁹ pairs per cm³, stacked in thin films between diamond collectors that turn the helium nuclei's energy straight into current. One on-board laser lights hundreds of tiles.

It assumes near-future atomic-level fabrication, and needs it only for the cage itself. The open question is the physics: can a pair at rest be made to fuse often enough?

Where it stands

ESTABLISHED

The gap is 53 orders of magnitude, and one lever, electron screening, controls nearly all of it. The model reproduces spectroscopy and the published D₂ rate.

RULED OUT

As power sources: a beam into cold matter, and a quiet lattice in equilibrium. As drives: light used as a hammer or as a holding field.

OPEN

Whether the screening seen in beam experiments acts on a pair at rest. Whether a scattered beam of bare ions can be recycled.

NEXT

A battery is a vast number of fuel pairs, each fusing rarely, so everything reduces to a rate per pair. The rate is set by how tightly electrons screen the two nuclei: over 22 pm in a molecule, against about 1 pm needed. Deuterons fired into metals behave as if screened that tightly. Whether a pair at rest is too is the question.

Deuterium is the fuel for proving the physics; it makes neutrons, so it stays in the laboratory. Lithium and boron are clean, and at rest they need screening nearly as tight as a muon's orbit (0.26 pm). They suit the routes that give the proton real kinetic energy.

What light can and cannot do

A battery returning three times its electricity can spend at most 43,000 photons per fusion.

✕ KNOCK, PUMP, HOLD

A 2 eV photon gives a deuteron 10⁻⁹ eV. The whole vibrational ladder buys 8 of the 53 orders. A held field costs 8 to 16 orders more than a site can repay.

✓ WRITE, TRIGGER, PROBE

Affordable: set a long-lived state and leave, switch at low duty, read out. No nuclear effect of low-power light is confirmed yet.

✓ PUSH UNLIKE PAIRS

A light field acts on p–Li and p–B pairs, and not at all on D–D.
