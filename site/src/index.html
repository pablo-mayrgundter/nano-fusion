Concept study · screening level · Milestone 1, November 2026

NanoFusion

a fusion battery, one reaction site at a time · modelling only, no hardware

One milligram of hydrogen and boron-11 holds 19 kWh, as much as 75 kg of lithium-ion cells, and fusing it leaves only helium. NanoFusion asks whether it could be burned in a solid chip instead of a plasma: a crystal of reaction sites, each built to atomic specification and holding a single fuel pair, 2.5 × 10¹⁹ pairs to the cubic centimetre, all switched by light from a shared on-board laser. A fusion battery with no plasma, no magnets and no moving parts.

This study works out what that would take. It comes down to one number with a long way to go. Two deuterium nuclei at rest in a molecule fuse at 2 × 10⁻⁶⁴ per second. A battery needs each pair to manage 10⁻¹¹ to 10⁻⁷. The 53 orders of magnitude in between are set almost entirely by how tightly the surrounding electrons screen the two nuclei from each other's charge: over about 22 picometres in a molecule, against the 1 to 1.5 a deuterium battery needs and the 0.3 to 0.6 needed by the clean fuels, lithium and boron.

Nothing known does that for a pair at rest. The one real lead is that deuterons fired into metals behave as if screened at about the right strength; whether that carries over to a resting pair is the question the first three years of the plan are built to settle. The study also closes some doors. Light cannot knock two nuclei together or hold a site active, though the budget still leaves it room to write, trigger and probe. A proton beam fired into solid boron returns 0.2 % of its energy. A perfectly aimed shot still misses a million times for every hit.

FRAMING. A screening-level desk study: models and published data, no hardware and no measurements of our own. The site model is benchmarked (the D₂ binding energy to 1 meV, the D₂ fusion rate within 1.5× of the published value), but a rate this sensitive to screening is only as good as the screening assumed, and the beam data it leans on have known artefacts. Device figures assume 20 % capture, a 30 % efficient laser and 50 % optical distribution. The odds quoted are a starting estimate, not a calculation.

The gap · one fuel pair at a time

Fifty-three orders of magnitude

A battery is an enormous number of fuel pairs, each fusing very rarely. That turns the whole question into a rate per pair: how often does one pair, sitting at rest in its site, fuse?

Site physics · one lever

How tightly the electrons screen

Two nuclei repel each other until the electrons around them cancel the charge. The shorter the distance over which that happens, the thinner the barrier left to tunnel through. Between a molecule's 22 pm and the 1 pm a battery needs, each halving is worth eight to nineteen orders of magnitude for deuterium, and more for the heavier fuels.

For deuterium the requirement lands on what beam experiments report. That coincidence is what the whole lattice idea rests on, and it is exactly where the doubt lies: a beam's screening is not known to act on a pair at rest, and a free electron gas cannot do it (palladium's screens over about 40 pm).

For the clean fuels it does not land anywhere near. They need sub-picometre screening, within a factor of 1.2 to 2.5 of a muon's orbit and ten times beyond anything reported for them. So deuterium is the fuel for proving the physics, and lithium and boron belong wherever the proton is given real kinetic energy.

Three routes · one collision

Three ways to make two nuclei meet

Every scheme has to bring two positive charges within a few femtometres of each other. They differ in who pays for the approach: nobody, an accelerator, or the aim.

A · wait

A pair at rest

{{way_rest}}

Put both nuclei in one cage and let them tunnel. Nothing is spent on the approach, so everything hangs on the rate: 2 × 10⁻⁶⁴ per second in a molecule, and 24 orders worse again if the two sit on neighbouring lattice sites instead of sharing one.

OPEN · THE LATTICE BETNeeds about 1 pm of screening for deuterium. Beam experiments report that strength; nobody has shown it for a pair at rest. Decided at Gate 1.
B · shoot

A beam into fuel

{{way_beam}}

Give a proton 650 keV and fire it into boron. It fuses readily when it hits, but the electrons take their 14 eV at every layer and a hit is rare: 0.19 % of the beam energy comes back, and no amount of energy recovery lifts that ceiling.

CAPPED IN MATTER · OPEN WITHOUT ELECTRONSStrip the electrons away and the ceiling rises to 1.2–1.6 on paper. The beam then scatters about a hundred times per fusion, so it works only if scattered ions are recycled at 90 to 99.9 %.
C · aim

The cue-ball shot

{{way_aim}}

Line up one proton on one nucleus. Head-on at 600 keV they fuse almost every time, and a single photon is a fine enough cue: it steers the proton by 6 fm over 100 nm of flight. But the nucleus jitters ±4.2 pm about its site even at absolute zero, 670 times the size of the pocket.

POSSIBLE · NOT ENOUGHA perfect shot hits about once in a million, 360 times better than an unaimed one, for a gain of 0.1 to 0.7 at best. Pinning the target would take 25 keV; a chemical bond supplies 55 meV.

The original notes' idea of grouping photons onto protons is route B with light as the accelerator. The single-photon cue ball is route C. The crystal of driven sites is route A.

Light · the photon budget

What light can and cannot do

The idea energises each site with photons from an on-board laser. One fusion of hydrogen and boron releases 8.7 MeV; a visible photon carries 2 eV. After capture and laser losses, a battery that returns three times the electricity it uses can spend at most 43,000 photons per fusion.

Ruled out

What the numbers close off, whatever the site looks like.

✕ KNOCKpush one nucleus into another

A deuteron that absorbs a 2 eV photon recoils with 10⁻⁹ eV. A 10 keV knock needs a gamma ray, or a field of 135,000 V/nm across the bond; materials fail near 10.

✕ PUMPshake a bonded pair together

Pumping a D₂ pair to the top of its vibrational ladder lifts the rate from 10⁻⁶⁴ to 10⁻⁵⁵ per second: 8 of the 53 orders, and then the bond breaks.

✕ HOLDkeep a site switched on

Holding one site in a 1 V/nm optical field costs 3 nW in the best cavity. The budget is 3 × 10⁻²¹ W per fuel pair. A held field is 8 to 16 orders of magnitude too expensive.

Still affordable

Roles the energy budget leaves open. None has a confirmed nuclear effect yet.

✓ WRITEset a long-lived state and leave

One photon per write pays off if the state it leaves outlasts the wait for a fusion: nine days at 10⁻¹¹ per second, about a minute at 10⁻⁷.

✓ TRIGGER, PROBEswitch and read at low duty

The first experiment fires deuterons at a palladium-deuteride antenna array while a laser is chopped on and off, and looks for a fusion yield that follows the light.

✓ PUSH UNLIKE PAIRSact on the clean fuels

A light field pulls a proton and a lithium or boron nucleus in opposite senses, with half an elementary charge of leverage. On two deuterons it does nothing at all.

The concept cell stacks 500 layers to the centimetre. Each is a 1 µm film of site crystal on a diamond collector, lit from below by a waveguide. One laser die feeds 625 tiles; a 1 W cell needs about 150 mW of light, which is one ordinary diode. Fuel is never the limit. What sets the life of a cell is helium, heat and radiation damage in its own collector, and at the handheld tier those bite within the hour.

Both cells are generated from YAML specifications by code-CAD and exported as STEP assemblies: a 2 × 2 pixel block of the laboratory cell, every antenna a solid, and the laser unit with its 625 tiles. Both load in Bldrs Share with instancing, colours and part names intact: open the laser unit or the pixel block in the browser, download the STEP files (laser unit, pixel block), or read the notes on the models.

Roadmap · 2027–2036

Ten years, built around gates

The plan does not assume the idea works. The first three years are designed to find out cheaply, and every phase ends in a product or a publishable result that survives a no.

{{timeline}}

BDriven lattice site

A dense crystal of caged fuel pairs, switched by on-chip light or sound. The flagship science, and unproven. Decided at Gate 1.

AScreened lattice and nano-accelerator

Ions of 1 to 100 keV fired into a designed lattice. Known physics, gain capped near 1 %. The metrology backbone and the first product.

DElectron-free nano-collider

Bare ions recirculating in atom-exact traps at 1 to 10 V/nm. The only known-physics path past break-even, and the likeliest home for p–¹¹B.

CMuon-catalysed cell

The 2014 concept. Known physics; the cost of a muon is the wall. Watched, and revisited if muon sources improve.

Rough odds, a starting estimate rather than a calculation: a confirmed change in a nuclear rate, controlled by structure or by light, at Gate 1: perhaps 1 in 5. Net electricity from a nano-cell by 2036, well under 1 in 100; from a clean-fuel lattice cell, lower again by an order of magnitude or more. A sellable chip-scale particle source and a designed-lattice nuclear metrology platform, better than even.

Across the study

What we learned, and what to watch

What we learned

  • One number decides the lattice route. The screening length has to fall from 22 pm to about 1 pm for deuterium. That single change is worth the whole 53 orders of magnitude.
  • The pair must share a cage. Deuterons on neighbouring sites in palladium deuteride fuse 24 orders more slowly than in a free molecule.
  • Deuterium proves the physics and cannot be the product. A 100 µW deuterium cell gives about 11 mSv per hour at 30 cm; the public limit is 1 mSv per year.
  • The clean fuels are muon-class hard at rest. Lithium and boron need 0.3 to 0.6 pm. They belong where the proton has real kinetic energy.
  • Light is a pen, not a hammer. It cannot knock, pump or hold. Inside a budget of 43,000 photons per fusion it could still write, trigger and probe, though no nuclear effect of low-power light has been confirmed.
  • Electrons are the tax on every beam. Solid boron returns 0.19 % of a beam's energy; bare nuclei 1.2 to 1.6 on paper, if a scattered beam can be recycled.
  • Aiming is not the problem. A photon steers finely enough. The target's own quantum jitter is 670 times wider than the pocket.

What to watch, and gates

Can an experiment tell? Gate 0 · Dec 2027

Pass if the simulator reproduces four of six published benchmarks and at least one experiment predicts a 10σ effect under the hypothesis and a clean null without it.

Does structure or light change a nuclear rate? Gate 1 · Dec 2029

The main decision. Pass on a change of 2× or more at fixed beam conditions, or any beamless nuclear signal at 5σ, replicated in two laboratories. Fail, and the lattice track goes to the watch-list.

Does it survive the fuel switch? Phase 2 · 2030–31

The pair model says lithium needs about 8 times and boron about 17 times the screening energy that deuterium does.

Can a collector survive its own alphas? Gate 3 · Dec 2033

At the handheld tier the assumed damage limit is reached in under an hour. Capture is measured, not assumed, before anything is scaled.
